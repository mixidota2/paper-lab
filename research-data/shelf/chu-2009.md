# Chu 2009: exclude from realized-uplift evidence

## Identity and provenance
L. K. Chu, *An efficient approach for shelf space planning and display area allocation in convenience stores*. IEEE IE&EM 2009, pp.874–878. DOI 10.1109/ICIEEM.2009.5344195. Institutional record: https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/99862 . Primary body: https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/99862/1/Content.pdf . Accessed 2026-10-29 10:56 UTC; all five pages parsed and read. PDF figures were not visually inspected.

## Evidence extract (concise paraphrase)
Hong Kong convenience-chain data collection lasted three months; 134 products, 30 categories, three departments (pp.876,878). Dates, store count, randomization and concurrent controls are unspecified. Trials varied facings/positions; weekly-demand ratios estimated responses. Genetic optimization selected facings then positions; simplified model ignored cross-effects (pp.875–876). No held-out predictive validation is reported.

Actual baseline weekly gross profit: HK$4,550 average, HK$5,202 maximum. Recommended grouped allocation: HK$5,681.53, approximately 25%/9% above those baselines; these are modeled comparisons, not observed implementation gains (p.877). The same page subsequently says 20%, without explaining reconciliation. Shelf-length constraints are exceeded and adjustments precede implementation. Store-A shelf length is 1,579.5 cm; conclusion reports 3,159 cm, without a clear bridge (pp.876–878).

## Interpretation
This is empirical response-estimation/offline-optimization evidence. It does not establish causal gains from fixed-total category allocation or subsequent realized profit. Traffic, seasonality, price changes and substitution are not adequately controlled or documented here; no uncertainty interval supports the headline gain.

## Arithmetic (independent, not validation)
5681.53 / 4550 - 1 = 24.8688%; 5681.53 / 5202 - 1 = 9.2182%. Arithmetic corroborates the approximate comparisons, not a realized effect.
